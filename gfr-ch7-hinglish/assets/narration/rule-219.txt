Rule 219 — Disposal through Advertised Tender.

Yaad rakhne ka formula: 9 steps (a)–(i) · 7 aspects (a)–(g) · 10% bid security · late bid = no.

Rule 219 teen hisson mein hai: (i) nau broad steps (a) se (i), (ii) saat important aspects (a) se (g), aur (iii) late bids ka rule.

Clause (i): Nau kadam: bidding document banana → tender invite karna → bids kholna → analysis/evaluation → highest responsive bidder chunna → sale value collect karna → sale release order dena → goods release karna → unsuccessful bidders ki bid security wapas karna. Aakhri step aksar bhool jaate hain — unsuccessful bidders ki security return karna bhi rule ka hissa hai.

Clause (ii)(a): Chaar value: transparency, competition, fairness, discretion ka khatma. Sale plan aur goods ka wide publicity zaroori. Saari terms bidding document mein comprehensive, plain and simple language mein honi chahiye — chhupe hue conditions nahi. Aur taxes ka applicability document mein saaf likha ho.

Clause (ii)(b): Bidding document mein goods ka location aur present condition dono likhne honge, taaki bidder bidding se pehle inspect kar sake. Isse "jo dekha wo mila" wala dispute nahi hota — isliye condition chhupana rule ke khilaf hai.

Clause (ii)(c): Bidders ko bid ke saath bid security deni hogi. Amount generally (ordinarily) assessed ya reserved price ka 10% hoga, aur exact amount bidding document mein likhna hi hoga. "Ordinarily" ka matlab hai ki yahi normal hai, par exact figure document mein declared hona chahiye — bidder ko andaza nahi lagwana chahiye.

Clause (ii)(d): Normal rule: highest acceptable responsive bidder ka bid accept hoga. Agar uska price acceptable na ho to negotiation sirf usi bidder se ho sakti hai — sab bidders se nahi. Agar negotiation fail ho jaaye, to reasonable/acceptable price ko counter-offer ke roop mein agle highest responsive bidder(s) ko diya ja sakta hai. Yani H1 ke saath negotiation ke baad H2 par jaane ka raasta hai, lekin H1 ke bina H2 ke saath negotiation nahi.

Clause (ii)(e): Agar H1 poori quantity nahi le sakta, to bachi hui quantity agle bidder(s) ko offer ki ja sakti hai — par usi price par jo H1 ne offer kiya tha. Matlab H2 ko H1 ke rate par milega, apne kam rate par nahi. Ye government ke liye price protection hai.

Clause (ii)(f): Poora payment — bid security adjust karke baaki jo bacha — goods release karne se pehle milna chahiye. "Udhaar mein maal de denge" Rule 219 ke khilaf hai.

Clause (ii)(g): Agar selected bidder maal uthane mein interest na dikhaaye (default), to bid security forfeit hogi, aur aage ki action hogi — jismein goods ki re-sale defaulter ke risk and cost par shaamil hai, legal advice lene ke baad. Yani default ka nuksan government ko nahi, defaulter ko jhelna hoga.

Clause (iii): Late bids — jo specified date/time ke baad aayein — consider nahi kiye jayenge. Ye Rule 202 (non-consulting services) jaisa hi absolute rule hai; koi discretion nahi.

Note. (i) mein nau steps hain (a)–(i), jabki (ii) mein saat aspects (a)–(g) — dono ki numbering alag hai, confuse na karein. Bid security "ordinarily 10%" hai, par exact amount bidding document mein likhna hi hoga.

Exception. (ii)(e) quantity-splitting ek MAY hai — zaroori nahi ki bachi quantity agle bidder ko hi jaaye.

Exam trap: Bid security kitni? Ordinarily assessed ya reserved price ka 10% — exact amount bidding document mein.

Exam trap: Negotiation kis se ho sakti hai? Sirf highest acceptable responsive bidder se (H1). Fail hone par H2 ko counter-offer.
